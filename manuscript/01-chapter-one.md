# The Letter

The package arrived on a Tuesday, which Vera considered suspicious. Good
things never arrived on Tuesdays.

It sat on her desk — brown paper, no postage, tied with waxed string — while
she made coffee and tried to convince herself she was not the kind of person
who received mysterious packages. The coffee finished brewing. The package
remained.

She cut the string.

Inside, wrapped in a square of green baize, was a device roughly the size and
shape of a pocket watch, though far heavier than any watch she had ever held.
Its case was brass, engine-turned, and along its rim ran a ring of tiny keys,
each engraved with a letter of the alphabet. A card, tucked beneath the baize,
read:

> *Wind it once. Write what it tells you. Stop when it stops.*
>
> *— A Friend*

Vera turned the device over in her palm. A hairline seam ran around its
equator. She found the crown — a small ridged knob at the three-o'clock
position — and wound it, counting twelve turns until it would wind no further.

Something inside began to tick.

She sat down, opened her notebook to a fresh page, and waited.

---

The first word arrived not as a sound but as a sensation: a faint vibration
in her writing hand, as though a thought had been placed there from outside.
She wrote it down.

*Marguerite.*

Then another. *Marguerite had not expected to outlive the lighthouse.*

She kept writing. The device kept ticking. Outside, the Tuesday rain began.
